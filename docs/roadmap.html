What It Does,
And What Comes Next

The deliverable is the simulator — Unreal Engine 5.8 + Cosys-AirSim + PX4 SITL + ROS 2 Jazzy. Bring your own Unreal world, place the vehicle, choose your sensors, and fly it over the same ROS 2 graph you would fly on real hardware. It flies, and it now looks right — an unmodified controller reaches 4/4 waypoints, and the imagery matches Unreal’s own render to 1.15 of 255. What the world still lacks is people and traffic.

The goals, in order

  1. A photoreal simulator that flies, on your world — not on a scene we picked for you.
  2. Reproducible as Docker from this repo alone — plus exactly one documented credential step, named in §04.
  3. Reuse upstream, don’t reinvent — PX4, Cosys-AirSim, ROS 2 and the Isaac ROS packages are pinned and wrapped; the glue is the original work.
  4. Sim-to-real parity — one ROS 2 graph, and only the transport is swapped.

What people build on it — vision-based navigation, VLM agents, planners, perception, benchmark reproduction — are applications, not the deliverable. The simulator has to be good on its own terms first.

It flies, and the parity claim is demonstrated rather than argued

The unmodified offboard_control node — the one written against the retired Gazebo baseline — reaches 4/4 waypoints in Cosys-AirSim on UE5.8, with errors 0.78 / 0.79 / 0.78 / 0.78 m, reproduced three times including from a cold start. Peak −10.233 m NED against a 10 m target. The controller was never patched; only the transport was swapped.

It was proved first as a diff: 51 /fmu/ topics, 24 /fmu/out, byte-identical between the Gazebo stack and Unreal. The perception graph runs alongside it — 19 /airsim_node/ topics carrying 640×480 RGB, depth, camera_info that resolves in TF, and an 8192-point GPU-LiDAR cloud, all checked by value rather than by topic presence.

Your own world loads, and the vehicle can be placed in it

scripts/inject_airsim.py takes a user’s .uproject and injects AirSim with no compile, no editor, no GUI and no display — four text edits and a folder copy. Proved against a project that was never ours: City Park declares EngineAssociation: "4.24", a UE4 project from 2019, and it loaded headless in UE5.8 with no conversion dialog and no loader errors — 856 scene objects, real trees, foliage and a stone stairway in the frames.

./scripts/sim_up.sh --spawn X,Y,Z[,YAW] then puts the vehicle where the operator wants it, and the pose holds without re-assertion — 0.000 m drift over 6 s unheld, which retired a workaround that had confounded four separate investigations.

One honest qualification. A UE4-era project loads correctly; it does not necessarily render correctly — City Park carries the deprecated AtmosphericFog actor with no SkyAtmosphere, and UE4-era post-process volumes. That is what an editor conversion pass fixes, and it can be done entirely on Linux, headless: UnrealEditor-Cmd -run=ResavePackages -IGNORECHANGELIST upgraded all 11 packages from UE version 518 to 1018 in ~2 minutes. No Windows machine is required for any of it.

The imagery is photorealistic, and that is a measurement

simGetImages matches Unreal’s own render of the same camera actor at the same transform to 1.15 of 255, across six scenes from close-up to 70 m. The long-running “the capture looks washed out” turned out to be three unrelated faults wearing one costume:

  • the measurement client read the buffer as BGR when Cosys-AirSim returns RGB — which does not look like a bug, it looks like a plausible cyan cast;
  • the camera was inside world geometry, and a near surface blurred by depth-of-field is itself a film over everything behind it;
  • PIPCamera.cpp:701-715 does not merely skip Lumen when the default is false — it forces global illumination and reflections to None, in a world authored for Lumen.

The fix is three settings.json keys on a stock plugin binary — LumenGIEnable, LumenReflectionEnable, ForceUpdate — costing ~8.6% RGB and ~10% LiDAR throughput on the real graph. An acceptable trade, and the frame rate is capped by the launch file anyway.

Commanding it is confirmed, not assumed

Three command kinds, each proven by the vehicle moving rather than by a publisher existing: local waypoint 0.98 m against a 1.0 m tolerance, velocity 2.00 m/s against 2.0 commanded, and a GPS waypoint that travelled 27.0 m of 30 m commanded with 2.99 m remaining. The failure mode this guards against is not a crash — it is a planner emitting setpoints PX4 quietly ignores while the flight looks fine.

The finding that shapes any planner built on top: there is no global setpoint message. GotoSetpoint is local NED and VehicleGlobalPosition is an estimate output, so a GPS waypoint cannot be streamed the way a local one is — it is a one-shot VehicleCommand that PX4 flies in its own navigation mode. Two control paths, different failsafes, not to be mixed casually.

A runnable example ships with it: SIM-16 flies a closed circuit of a world using only ROS 2, in waypoint or smooth-orbit mode, and records the run to MCAP with a ground-track plot and a node/topic graph — the same artifact layout the Gazebo gate wrote.

Three things that cost real time, and are worth carrying forward
  • A stale PX4 EKF origin made the vehicle report 35.167 m of altitude while parked on the ground, which presents exactly as a control bug and cost a day. scripts/sim_up.sh now verifies and repairs the origin on every bring-up — on its first honest cold start it caught a fresh one at 9.069 m, restarted PX4, re-verified at 0.000 m apart, and the stack then flew 4/4. The gate VOIDs such runs rather than scoring them as failures.
  • Lockstep is confirmed dead code in Cosys-AirSim — the flag is set once and cleared twice, and the activation message never fires. "LockStep": true is silently ineffective, so every timing number here is free-running and none may be quoted as deterministic.
  • The AirSim ROS 2 wrapper aborted on first publish from an upstream data race — a Reentrant callback group on a MultiThreadedExecutor, which made clean strings serialise as if they contained NULs. One-word fix, carried as a recorded patch; vendor/ stays byte-identical to upstream.
Two corrections worth more than the fixes they came from

A negative result was VOID because the artifact under test was never loaded. Unreal de-duplicates plugins by name+version and had silently preferred a stale backup left inside Plugins/. The md5 check that “verified” the build inspected the file on disk, not the one the engine resolved. Verifying that an artifact exists is not verifying it was used.

The “31 Hz RGB / 29.6 Hz depth / 17.4 Hz LiDAR” figure is retired. It does not reproduce — stock settings now measure 18.7 / 18.1 / 10.0 Hz on the same grey-box world. It predates perception.launch.py, which pins the poll periods at 20 Hz imagery and 10 Hz LiDAR, so today’s numbers sit at 94% and 100% of their ceilings. The clearest tell: 17.4 Hz from a sensor configured RotationsPerSecond: 10 was reporting poll rate, not data rate. Nothing regressed; the old number measured the wrong thing.

03 What is next

Next: SIM-11 — actors in the user’s world

The world is photorealistic and the drone can be placed in it deliberately. What it has none of is people, vehicles or moving obstacles — nothing to navigate around, which is the other half of “a photorealistic simulator with actors”.

The path needs no project C++ and no plugin change. The RPC surface (simListAssets, simSpawnObject, simSetObjectPose, simSetObjectScale, simDestroyObject, plus simAddDetectionFilterMeshName for ground-truth labels) works in any project, preserving the no-compile drop-in property that makes bring-your-own-world work at all. simGetObjectPose was exercised against City Park on 2026-08-03, so that surface is known live in this build rather than assumed.

One decision to make deliberately: the vendored -startSeed/-spawnAI determinism lives on the C++ side — adding a Source/ module to a user’s project turns the no-compile case into the compile case, breaking bring-your-own-world for exactly the feature the gate needs. Seed it in our own scenario harness instead (seeded RNG → spawn poses → record the seed): reproducible in any world, no vendor change, and squarely the “glue is the original work” split this project is built on. Reimplementing a vendor feature normally deserves suspicion; here the vendor’s version is unusable without giving up the drop-in property.

First step, deliberately small: spawn a few actors into City Park over RPC, confirm they appear in both RGB and GPU-LiDAR, and re-measure rates with actors present — RGB already sits at 17.1 Hz against a 20 Hz cap. (The old “the segfault gets likelier with more actors” caveat is withdrawn — it never had a measurement behind it, and a 90-minute full-stack soak did not reproduce the crash at all.)

Ground-truth labels

Half of this is already in the graph: instance_segmentation_labels and object_transforms publish today, and a settings-level detection filter is available over RPC. What is unproven is that the labels track spawned actors and survive a user world. The surface itself is live — a scene enumerated 255 instance-segmentation names, none malformed — but nothing has yet been trained or evaluated against them.

Worth stating because it decides what the labels are good for: odom_local is sim ground truth from the physics engine, not an estimate. On the real Pixhawk 6C the equivalent is a noisy EKF output. Tuning against noiseless odometry is a sim-to-real gap that will not surface until the real flight.

The seeded scenario harness — and what a seed does not do yet

scripts/run_scenario.py now drives scripts/sim_up.sh directly. A seed currently controls the SPAWN POSE and nothing else. The retired Gazebo harness seeded wind and vehicle mass through a generated world overlay, and there is no equivalent here yet — it needs Cosys-AirSim’s wind API, which is part of SIM-07.

So gate runs must not be described as covering varied conditions. They cover varied placement. Saying otherwise would inflate exactly the number the gate exists to keep honest.

SIM-07 — the flight gate, and wind/environment control

scripts/run_gate.py is now the simulator’s gate, keeping the scoring semantics it was given: a VOID run is excluded from the success rate (it never measured the flight code) and separately blocks the criterion — excluding without blocking would let a gate where 9 of 10 runs were void report 100%. Scoring lives in a pure score() so the semantics are unit-tested: all-void is not a pass, an empty run list is not a pass, and a real failure is still a failure.

What it still needs: genuinely varied conditions, which is where wind, time of day and weather come in (simSetWind, simSetTimeOfDay, simEnableWeather — all RPC, all available in any world). Then a success rate over seeded runs, with the per-seed table and an MCAP per run.

Two traps carried forward. The Gazebo gate took ~19 min for ten seeds and already missed a 10-minute CI budget; a UE5 stack starts slower, not faster — decide the seed count with evidence and do not fit a budget by quietly weakening the gate. And the gate needs a barrier before its origin check: a version without one passed 10/10 by timing coincidence, not by construction.

SIM-14 — automatic spawn siting

Unblocked for free by the operator spawn: spawn-and-settle is a ground probe. Release the vehicle high at some (X, Y), read simGetObjectPose("PX4"), and the resting Z is the terrain surface — it returned Z ≈ +15.9 first time at City Park. Settings plus RPC only; no plugin change, which was the open question it was blocked on.

Ground height alone is not enough, and that is measured too: the surveyed coordinate landed exactly where commanded and was still useless, because it was scrub on a slope. A depth frame scores openness directly — frac(depth < 0.5 m) read 0.32 in undergrowth and 1.00 fully embedded, against near zero for a clear view.

HITL, and the boundary that matters

The last transport swap is the real one: PX4 on the Pixhawk 6C, the same px4_msgs over uXRCE-DDS, the same graph. Everything on this page is SITL, and it stays that way until an operator approves a specific run — approval never carries over, not to a retry, not to the next scenario.

One thing to settle before then, still open: the bring-up has been verified once, not N times in a row. The 5-reads-at-0.05 m settle heuristic was chosen, not derived.

04 Deferred, and the two real blockers

Blocker 1 — NVENC is unavailable on driver 610.43.03

Video capture out of the simulator is capped at ~13–14 Hz at any resolution. Every route AirSim offers funnels through RenderRequest::getScreenshot, which waits for the next rendered frame and then does a blocking GPU→CPU readback:

capturedatatime per framerate
960×5401.56 MB71.1 ms14.1 Hz
1920×10806.22 MB96.9 ms10.3 Hz

Those two points separate the costs: ~71 ms fixed, ~5 ms/MB. Four times the data costs only 26 ms more, so it is latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound. Through the ROS 2 wrapper and a rosbag it falls to 4.69 Hz. Real-time factor stayed 1.0 throughout, so the engine is not the limit — the round trip is.

The fix is Pixel Streaming with GPU-side encode, and NVENC cannot open an encode session on this host: OpenEncodeSessionEx failed: unsupported device. Libraries mounted, device nodes present, nvidia-smi healthy, CUDA initialises — the encoder alone refuses. UE 5.8’s only NVIDIA encoder backend is NVENC, so the fallback is software VP8, which is CPU-bound and needs frames in system memory, reintroducing the readback the whole task exists to remove. Evidence and ruled-out causes: docs/nvenc-driver-blocker.md.

What this does and does not cost. It blocks presentation-quality 1080p60 video. Nothing that flies is affected — perception keeps 640×480 in ROS 2 at its capped rate, in the bag, undisturbed. Interim: 960×540 at ~14 Hz, ~3× smoother than 1080p’s 4.69 Hz. Resolving it is an owner decision (a host driver rebase), and the same change is what Isaac Sim would need — two capabilities lost to one driver.

Blocker 2 — the Unreal base image is credential-gated

ghcr.io/epicgames/unreal-engine denies anonymous reads (HTTP 403, DENIED: invalid token). Pulling dev-slim-5.8.0 needs EpicGames GitHub org membership plus a PAT with read:packages — and it is 24.0 GB compressed across 30 layers, on an Ubuntu 22.04 base, which is why the renderer and the ROS 2 side are separate containers rather than stylistically separate: ROS 2 Jazzy has no jammy packages, so nothing Jazzy can be installed inside the engine image.

This is upstream licensing, not a gap to close, so the goal is restated rather than quietly failed: a fresh machine reaches a working stack from the repo alone plus one documented credential step — documented, scripted where possible, and named in the README rather than discovered. Anything less and the honest description of this project would be “reproducible except for the simulator”. Inspecting the pin is cheap, at least: the gh CLI token exchanges for a read-only registry bearer, so the tag can be confirmed for ~17 KB without touching the 24 GB.

Pin the three-component tag, never dev-slim-5.8. Both resolve to the same digest today, but the two-component form is a moving alias — the registry shows dev-slim-5.5 and dev-slim-5.5.4 sharing one digest, i.e. -5.5 tracked four patch releases.

Deferred on purpose, with the reason recorded

SIM-12 — the capture carries more high-frequency speckle than Unreal’s render. ForceUpdate removed the Lumen-attributable part (−13.9%, free), and a Lumen-off control proved that is exactly what it denoises. The residual number is not trustworthy: ~17.6 against native ~7.0, and it survives 2× supersampling (−3.5% for 2.3× the frame-rate cost), which real edge aliasing would not — so much of the gap is likely native’s TSR softening the reference. The first task there is a better metric, not a better fix, and it may well close as won’t-fix if feature trackers turn out to be indifferent to it.

SIM-05 — cuVSLAM and nvblox, deprioritised by the owner, not blocked. Worth stating plainly because the names collide: Isaac ROS is not Isaac Sim. These are ROS 2 Jazzy packages that consume image and depth topics from any source, so retiring the Isaac Sim renderer cost the project nothing here. What this will inherit: the IMU is a polled snapshot rather than a stream, which is the bad case for a preintegrating VIO, and frames arrive NWU, not ENU, with a tested conversion in control/frames.py that nothing calls yet.

SIM-08 — Cesium georeferenced terrain and OSM, deliberately later: georeferencing is benchmark reproduction, not photorealism. Cesium v2.28.0 supports UE5.8 and v2.29.0 drops UE5.5, which is part of why the engine pin is where it is.

Known limits, stated rather than buried
  • Lockstep is dead code — never quote a real-time factor from this simulator as deterministic.
  • Frame rate is capped by the launch file, not the hardware — 20 Hz imagery, 10 Hz LiDAR, measured throughput at 94% and 100% of those ceilings.
  • The IMU rate is a poll rate. ~333 Hz is a ceiling and a fraction of messages carry duplicate timestamps; ros2 topic hz reports a healthy-looking number and tells you none of it. (The once-quoted 77.9% duplicate rate came from five uninitialised timer periods in the wrapper, and was fixed in the launch file — the polled design remains.)
  • An unexplained 7.342° yaw residual in the frame conversion, measured and open.
  • One simulator segfault at n = 1, after ~57 minutes. A deliberate 90-minute soak of the full stack with a concurrent RPC load ran 74,253 captures with zero anomalies and refuted both hypotheses — upstream’s “every 2000 or so calls” (wrong by >30×) and the ~57-minute interval. Not reproduced is not fixed.

This page previously stated that a personal access token pasted into a session had not been rotated. That claim was investigated and did not hold — no bearer value appeared in any transcript, the credential files were 0600, and the credential in use was an existing gh login. It is corrected here rather than quietly deleted, because a false security claim left standing is its own kind of harm.

Also corrected: the merge gate has now run on the open work, and its findings landed with the rest of PR 33; the vendoring notes exist at docs/vendor/cosys-airsim.md; and the in-tree build artifacts are out of vendor/. 06 How we got here

This repo used to carry three simulators; it now carries one. The retired backlogs and design docs live under docs/history/, and the dated record of how the work actually happened — including its original wording, deliberately left alone — is docs/worklog/. The short version, without re-telling it:

  • The Gazebo baseline (PX4 v1.16 + Gazebo Harmonic + ROS 2 Jazzy) is where the offboard controller, the frame conventions, the MCAP artifact layout, the gate’s scoring semantics and the off-target test suite were built. The simulator inherited all of it unchanged — the controller that flies in Unreal is byte-identical to the one that scored 10/10 there. That success rate is evidence about Gazebo and does not transfer, which is exactly why SIM-07 exists. Keeping it alive during bring-up paid for itself: four of the five failures hit while standing the Unreal stack up were already written down in its notes.
  • Isaac Sim + Pegasus never worked on this bench. Isaac Sim 5.1 SIGSEGVs on driver 610.43.03 against its validated 580.65.06 — reproduced on the Ampere card with the Blackwell one excluded, so it is the driver and not the GPU — and no Pegasus release exists for Isaac 6.0, which would have meant writing the PX4 bridge ourselves. Its photoreal role is now the simulator’s own. A host rebase to an R580 driver would reopen it, and would also unblock NVENC.
  • The frame conventions survived the move intact. docs/conventions.md was promoted out of the retired backlog because it is still an active spec: ENU/FLU outside, NED inside, converted in exactly one tested place. The simulator does not get to invent a second convention — it just has to reach the first one from NWU.
