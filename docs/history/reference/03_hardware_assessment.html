Go/no-go assessment of the existing workstation against the triple-lane simulation stack, with a recommended GPU work-assignment, VRAM budgets per component, and a downscale ladder if it does not clear.

Frozen — historical record

This page is archived and preserved exactly as written. It is not maintained. It assesses this workstation against the project's original three-stack design. Much of it is scoped to Isaac Sim, a stack this repo no longer contains, and the machine's NVIDIA driver has since moved past the versions discussed here.

Read it as evidence, never as instructions. The stack names, task IDs and component layout on this page refer to things that have since been renamed or deleted. Every figure is preserved unchanged — including this document's own caveat that the VRAM numbers are order-of-magnitude planning figures from forum reports, not measurements.

Its GPU work split is still the operating rule — render on the RTX 3080, infer on the 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti — which is the main reason this page is kept.

The repo today is one simulator: Unreal Engine 5.8 + Cosys-AirSim + PX4 SITL + ROS 2 Jazzy. What is still worth reading here: docs/history/README.md · task-ID renames: id-map.md · the repo: README.md

§ 1 · VERDICT

Go / no-go

Headline Qualified yes — the stack holds up, provided the RTX 3080 is the renderer and the RTX 5060 Ti is the inference card. Do not do it the other way round. The 5060 Ti is Blackwell (sm_120) and carries documented Isaac Sim startup crashes on recent driver branches; the Ampere 3080 is a proven, stable RTX renderer with roughly twice the shader throughput.

Two things that change this answer VRAM variant. If your 3080 is the 10 GB card and your 5060 Ti is the 8 GB card, you are below spec on both — the assessment above assumes at minimum a 10 GB 3080 and prefers a 16 GB 5060 Ti. Storage. Unspecified. Isaac assets, a UE5 project and rosbags each run to tens or hundreds of GB — provision ≥1 TB NVMe or this becomes the silent bottleneck.

The 3080 has roughly 1.9× the CUDA cores and 1.7× the memory bandwidth, and lands about 10–13% ahead on aggregate compute despite being four years older. The 5060 Ti's newer feature set — 4th-gen RT, FP4/FP8 tensor cores, DLSS 4 — buys nothing for this workload, because Isaac's RTX renderer and RTX-sensor ray tracing are throughput- and bandwidth-bound and none of the sim components use FP4 or DLSS.

§ 3 · RISK

The Blackwell problem

Primary build risk Isaac Sim on RTX 50-series can be broken by a driver that is too new. Isaac Sim 5.1/6.0 validate against Linux driver 580.65.06. Forum and GitHub reports cluster startup crashes on the 595.xx branch — rtx.scenedb.plugin crashes, TLAS limit errors, and CUDA-device detection failures — with the documented fix being a driver downgrade. One report on a 5070 hits Warp CUDA error: Failed to get driver entry point 'cuDeviceGetUuid' even on 580.65.06.
LayerBlackwell statusRequirement
NVIDIA driverSingle driver serves bothR580 branch validates CUDA 13.x across Maxwell → Blackwell. No fundamental mixed-architecture conflict.
Isaac Sim 5.1 / 6.0Runs, fragileValidated on 580.65.06; reports of invalidated behaviour past 580 on 5.1.0.
Isaac Lab / PyTorchNeeds cu128sm_120 requires CUDA 12.8+ and PyTorch built with sm_120 kernels. Stock wheels hang before printing the model structure; the documented fix is the cu128 nightly.
Isaac ROS (cuVSLAM, nvblox)Verify per releasex86_64 discrete NVIDIA GPUs; ESS stereo requires sm_80+. Both your cards clear that floor. Blackwell viability depends on the CUDA/TensorRT version inside the container.
vLLMFineBlackwell inference is well-supported; FP4/FP8 is a bonus, not a requirement.
Why the assignment de-risks this Routing all rendering to the Ampere 3080 removes Blackwell from the Isaac Sim critical path entirely. The 5060 Ti then only ever runs vLLM, where Blackwell support is solid. The build stays robust regardless of how the Isaac Sim / Blackwell situation evolves.
§ 4 · ASSIGNMENT

Recommended GPU work split

Pinning, precisely

# Isaac Sim — kit flags. Index comes from the Omniverse .log [gpu.foundation]
# table, NOT from nvidia-smi ordering.
./isaac-sim.sh --/renderer/activeGpu=0 --/physics/cudaDevice=0
./isaac-sim.sh --/renderer/multiGpu/enabled=false   # force single-GPU

# vLLM — honours the environment variable
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 vllm serve Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-AWQ \
  --quantization awq --port 8000
# note: `int4` is NOT a valid vLLM quantization value — use awq

Platform notes

  • PCIe. Consumer Core i9 exposes 16 (+4) CPU lanes; a dual-GPU board splits the primary slot to ×8/×8. The 5060 Ti is natively PCIe 5.0 ×8 regardless. Cost is low single-digit percent for rendering and effectively nil for bandwidth-bound inference — not a bottleneck.
  • Power. 320 W + 180 W of GPU plus a Core i9 at 125–253 W. Budget an 850–1000 W 80+ Gold, ATX 3.x supply.
  • Physical. Confirm two slots clear — the 3080 is frequently a 2.7-slot card.
§ 5 · BUDGET

VRAM footprint per component

The number that matters Isaac Sim's stated minimum is now 16 GB / RTX 4080. Isaac Sim 5.0, 5.1 and 6.0 all list the same tiers — minimum RTX 4080 16 GB, good RTX 5080 16 GB, ideal RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell 48 GB. This is a deliberate hike; 4.5.0 listed an 8 GB RTX 3070. A 10 GB 3080 sits below the current minimum. It will still run — NVIDIA staff separately recommend a practical floor of 12 GB — but scene complexity is your budget.

Single-thread latency, not core count PX4 SITL lockstep pins PX4 and the simulator to the same step. CPU starvation is a documented cause of the Accel #0 fail: TIMEOUT! and MAG #0 failed: TIMEOUT! errors from FM 01 § 10. A Core i9 has ample headroom — provided you are not simultaneously saturating every core with a UE5 shader compile. Shader compilation is heavily multithreaded and will starve the SITL loop.
§ 7 · FALLBACK

Downscale ladder

If Lane B does not hold, work down this list in order. Each step buys VRAM at a known cost in fidelity.

§ 8 · UPGRADE

Is an upgrade warranted?

Highest value

One 24 GB card

Replace the pair with a single 24 GB card. Clears the 16 GB Isaac minimum with headroom, removes mixed-architecture complexity, and lets Isaac Sim and an 8B VLM share one GPU.

Recommended if budget allows

Cheap interim

12 GB 3080 / 3080 Ti

Swap the 10 GB card for a 12 GB one. Clears NVIDIA's practical 12 GB floor, and the 384-bit bus at ~912 GB/s materially helps large scenes.

Good value

Do nothing

Keep both cards

If your workflow naturally splits sim from VLM and you never need one enormous scene, the task-partition model is genuinely good value as it stands.

Defensible

Thresholds that force the decision

  • Isaac Sim only ever runs headless or light scenes → both cards are overkill; the 3080 alone suffices.
  • You need Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B or larger locally → no combination of your current cards works. Buy 24 GB+ or go remote.
  • You need more than two RTX stereo pairs plus Lidar in one scene → 16 GB is the hard floor. Plan a single 16–24 GB card, not the 10 GB 3080.
§ 9 · SEQUENCE

Bring-up order

  1. Driver first, before anything else. Install the newest R580 production driver that still launches Isaac Sim. Verify Isaac Sim opens before installing the rest of the stack — a rtx.scenedb.plugin, TLAS, or cuDeviceGetUuid failure means your branch is incompatible. Then pin the driver and hold it.
  2. Identify GPU indices from the Omniverse .log [gpu.foundation] table. These are not nvidia-smi indices. Record both in versions.lock.
  3. Pin Isaac to the 3080 with --/renderer/activeGpu and --/physics/cudaDevice; disable multi-GPU rendering explicitly.
  4. Pin vLLM to the 5060 Ti with CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1. Start with Qwen3-VL-4B, measure, then step up to 8B if headroom allows.
  5. Validate Lane A first — it is CPU-bound and will confirm the host is healthy before you introduce GPU variables.

§ 11 · CAVEATS

What is not solid here

  • NVIDIA publishes no exact per-sensor or absolute scene VRAM figures. The idle (~3–5 GB) and small-scene (~3.2 GB) numbers are individual nvidia-smi reports on specific hardware. Isaac Sim 6.0's benchmark "GPU Memory Tracked" metric appears to undercount the true renderer plus texture-streaming footprint. Treat every VRAM figure here as an order-of-magnitude planning number, not a specification.
  • Qwen3-VL 2B/4B/8B serving sizes are derived from Q4_K_M GGUF weight sizes, which are comparable to AWQ but not identical, and come from a third-party guide rather than official Qwen benchmarks. Real vLLM totals run higher once the vision encoder loads and KV cache is pre-allocated.
  • The Blackwell crash reports are version-specific and moving. A newer Isaac Sim patch or driver may resolve them before you build. Re-check the IsaacSim issue tracker against your exact driver. Routing rendering to the 3080 makes the build robust either way.
  • Isaac Sim 5.1.0 is marked no longer supported in NVIDIA's docs, superseded by 6.0. FM 01 and FM 02 specify 5.1 because Pegasus v5.1.0 is pinned to it. Migrating to 6.0 means Python 3.12, which changes the ROS 2 integration path described in FM 02 § 4.
  • RTX 3080 laptop variants ship 8–16 GB at lower TDP. This assessment assumes desktop cards; if yours is mobile, the low-VRAM warnings bite harder.
