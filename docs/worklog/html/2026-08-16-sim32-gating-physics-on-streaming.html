01 Why gate physics at all

On a World Partition world the terrain streams in around the vehicle, and the vehicle starts falling on the first physics tick. Everything before this either raced that — patch 0005, registering the pawn as a streaming source, necessary but not sufficient — or reacted to it, which is SIM-30’s pause-and-probe: it works, and it is a workaround made of timing constants.

The gate is the fix. PhysicsWorld is constructed with start_async_updator = false, and ASimModeWorldBase::Tick starts the updator once the world says streaming has settled — or once a 300 s deadline expires, because a gate with no escape is a hang, which is worse than the race it replaces.

A wrong conclusion, corrected

I recorded that the timeout never fired. It does. Held at z = −8.000, vz = 0.000 for ~38 s, then released, fell, settled at z = 0.698. I had called the escape hatch broken without waiting long enough for it.

02 The first cut asked the wrong question

IsStreamingCompleted(nullptr) means “every streaming level in the world”. On CitySample it never went true — a world with continuously streaming traffic and crowds may simply never be globally settled — so every bring-up paid the full timeout rather than releasing when the ground was ready. Correct, and uselessly slow.

The scoped form asks about the cells under the vehicle instead: one FWorldPartitionStreamingQuerySource per vehicle at PawnSimApi::getUUPosition(), with bUseGridLoadingRange = true so the radius comes from the world’s own streaming grid rather than a number invented here.

wp->IsStreamingCompleted(EWorldPartitionRuntimeCellState::Activated, sources, /*bExactState=*/false)

Activated is the state a cell must reach before its collision exists, which is the whole point of waiting. The enum’s header and the static_cast<PawnSimApi*> idiom were both grepped in the engine and plugin trees rather than recalled.

03 A trap walked into by being careful

The backup was itself the shadow

Backing the plugin up as Plugins/AirSim.pristine.bak put a second AirSim.uplugin inside UE’s recursive scan root — confirmed by listing before and after, two AirSim uplugins existed. This is the same shadowing failure that has cost this project time before, reintroduced by the act of taking a backup. It now lives at worlds/.plugin-backups/, outside the scan root.

05 The other half, no longer a prediction

The run died on no finite EKF origin, with 166 poll timeout errors and PX4 reporting GPS Vertical Pos Drift too high and height estimate not stable. Holding physics starves PX4’s HIL sensor stream, and PX4 was started as soon as TCP 4560 accepted a connection — long before the gate had any verdict. SIM-10 is not a separate task from this one.

Confound worth naming

CitySample lives on the 7 TB spinning disk, which the project’s own rule says is the wrong place for the simulator’s live working set. Every duration above is inflated by that. It does not change the mechanism — the surrogate is Activated regardless of how fast the real cells arrive — but these are not the numbers this fix would post on NVMe.

Next, in order

1. Instrument instead of latching: log the query every tick for the whole bring-up rather than stopping at the first true. 2. Then decide the predicate — a dwell requirement is the obvious candidate, but only step 1 says whether a usable dwell is long enough to outlast the surrogate. 3. Fold in SIM-10. 4. Only then measure over N cold bring-ups, on NVMe.

06 Step 1: instrumented, and it rules the predicate out

Rebuilt so the query is evaluated every tick for 600 s, logging on change rather than latching on the first true — and so the verdict also goes out via UE_LOG, since it had been reaching only the Slate overlay. Second cold CitySample bring-up. The complete transition trace across the whole window:

[03.08.12] SIM-32 probe: streaming settled -> TRUE at 0.400 s

One transition. TRUE at 0.400 s, and never FALSE again in 600 s — a window that covers the period in which the previous run’s vehicle fell 32 m. The vehicle ended this run resting at z = +0.752 against +0.751 last run: the same fall-then-land sequence, reproduced to the millimetre.

A dwell requirement cannot fix this

Dwell was the leading candidate going in — wait for the query to hold true for N seconds. But dwell only helps if the signal changes, and this one does not. It reads TRUE continuously straight through the surrogate being replaced by the real city. Waiting longer on a constant adds latency and nothing else.

IsStreamingCompleted(Activated, {source at the vehicle}) answers “are the currently-requested cells activated”. That is true of the far-field surrogate and stays true as detail levels swap underneath. It has no notion of “the ground under me is the ground that will still be here in a minute”.

This is a negative result about the predicate, not the gate. The gate mechanism is sound and measured — physics holds, the escape releases. What has no working implementation is the condition. 0.400 s is deterministic: identical on two cold bring-ups.

Where that leaves it

Not “tune the dwell”. The predicate has to come from outside the streaming subsystem’s completion flag — a downward trace gated on the hit actor not being a FastGeoSurrogateActor, or waiting on the specific cell containing the spawn point. And a live option: don’t ship this. SIM-30’s pause-and-probe already works, and “better design, no working predicate” does not beat “working”.

07 Closed out — pause-and-probe is the official solution

Owner’s call: “let’s just log all of this effort and for now let’s use the simPause method as the official solution. We can check back on this in the future.” So SIM-30’s pause-and-probe is the answer to the falling vehicle — it ships, it works, and City Sample flies on it. SIM-32 stays on its branch as a recorded negative result — parked under patches/cosys-airsim/experimental/, which is where it had to be moved after review found the “applied to nothing” claim was false (see §08).

Read this first when picking it up again

This is most likely a slow-storage problem. CitySample lives on the 7 TB spinning disk, against this project’s own rule about the live working set. Both cold runs took ~5 minutes to a first frame, and the entire failure is what happens in the gap between the gate releasing at 0.400 s and the real city arriving.

Slow storage does not cause the predicate bug — it sets its blast radius. IsStreamingCompleted would still answer TRUE on the surrogate on the fastest disk ever made; that is semantics, not timing. What storage changes is how long the vehicle stands on an answer that is about to stop being true. On NVMe that window should shrink roughly with the load time — plausibly enough that the patch as written becomes good enough. Untested, and cheap to test: copy CitySample to the internal NVMe and re-run the same instrumented build. The probe already logs every transition, so the answer reads off docker logs in one line.

Banked

The gate mechanism is proven — physics holds at z = −0.000, the 300 s escape releases. The scoped query answers in 0.400 s, deterministic across two cold runs, against a nullptr query that never resolved. SIM-10 is coupled and demonstrated: 166 poll timeout errors, no finite EKF origin. And two defects fixed en route — the verdict never reached stdout, and a plugin backup shadowed the plugin.

Ruled out

IsStreamingCompleted as the release predicate. One transition to TRUE, never FALSE in 600 s, straight through the surrogate swap. A dwell requirement cannot fix a constant. The untested idea left on the table: a downward trace rejecting a FastGeoSurrogateActor hit — asking whether real ground is below, instead of asking the streaming subsystem a question it cannot answer.

08 Correction — “applied to nothing” was false

Caught by review, and the most important thing on this page

I stated repeatedly — here and in the PR — that patches/cosys-airsim/0007 was applied to nothing, and that nothing in the shipped bring-up path changed. That was wrong. build_blocks.sh:65 and convert_world.sh:154 both glob patches/cosys-airsim/*.patch and apply every patch whose diff touches Unreal/. 0007 matches.

APPLIES: 0005-worldpartition-streaming-source.patch
APPLIES: 0006-gpulidar-empty-readback.patch
APPLIES: 0007-gate-physics-on-streaming.patch

So the next world conversion would have silently shipped the gate — which by the measurements on this very page starves PX4’s HIL stream and kills bring-up. The safety property that justified merging a parked negative result at all did not hold.

How it was missed, since that is the reusable part. My own build output said applied  0007-gate-physics-on-streaming.patch, in this session, more than once. I read it as “the build applied it for my test” when it meant “the build applies it, always”. The evidence was in front of me and I fitted it to what I already believed.

Fixed by moving it to patches/cosys-airsim/experimental/, outside the non-recursive glob — the mechanism that already parks 0004, and which existed the whole time.

Five more defects, none fixed

Recorded in experimental/README.md as blockers for un-parking. Two change the design’s claim about itself: simPause(true) during the gate window is silently discarded, because startAsyncUpdator() runs initializePauseState() — colliding directly with SIM-30’s pause-and-probe, the very solution this work defers to. And continueForTime busy-waits on a flag only the unstarted executor can flip, blocking the game thread so Tick() never runs, the gate never releases, and the 300 s escape never fires. A hang — which the patch header explicitly claims to have designed against.

09 Second review pass — the evidence reads two ways

The headline measurement is double-edged

I recorded settled at z=-0.000 / on ground at z=-0.000 as proof the gate holds the vehicle. It is also proof that SIM-30’s probe was blind: with physics held, vz is exactly 0 — the very signal resting() uses — so its first check passes unconditionally and it reports “on ground” for any world at all. The gate silently disables the safety net it defers to. That reading did not occur to me while writing the measurement down, and it is the more important of the two.

My defect list had a mechanism backwards

I recorded that simPause(true) during the gate window would be silently discarded. It cannot be. ASimModeWorldBase::pause() calls physics_world_->pause() and ASimModeBase::pause(), which calls SetGamePaused (SimModeBase.cpp:1472); no AirSim actor sets bTickEvenWhenPaused, so while paused Tick() does not run. The real failure is the opposite and worse — the pause wedges the gate. ensure_grounded holds for up to 600 s against the gate’s 300 s deadline, and that deadline is accumulated DeltaSeconds, so it does not advance while paused either. A deadlock between the gate and the workaround that replaced it. I checked it myself after the review raised it, and my first reading of the source was wrong in the review’s favour.

Nine defects are now recorded in experimental/README.md. Also corrected: the vendor doc’s “only patch that would touch the UE plugin” callout, the defect count in three places, and SIM-30’s status — flipped to ✅ on an owner’s choice of approach when that entry’s own bar is “done means a success rate, not a success” and no N-run campaign has been run. It now reads 🟢 approach chosen, success rate not re-measured.
