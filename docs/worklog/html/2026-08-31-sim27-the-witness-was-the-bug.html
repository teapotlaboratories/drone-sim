The Witness
Was The Bug

For three weeks a landing that never terminated was blamed on the simulator’s ground detection. It was our own collision monitor, eating the one-shot flag the physics engine needed — and the fix is not to switch it off, because that breaks takeoff instead.

01 What was actually happening

watch_collisions.py is harness plumbing, not part of the aircraft: it polls the simulator 20 times a second so the gate can score whether a flight hit anything. The RPC it calls, simGetCollisionInfo, is read-and-reset — has_collided is one-shot, and the first reader clears it.

The physics engine needs that same flag to run collision response:

if (body.isGrounded() || (collision_info.has_collided && collision_response.collision_time_stamp != collision_info.time_stamp))

The witness kept winning the race. No flag → no collision response → no ground lock → the integrator descends through the world while the Unreal actor stays on the surface. Every PX4 sensor is derived from that integrator, so the aircraft is told it is still falling, never disarms, and the landing times out — reported, until last week, as a flight-control failure.

It also settles the rate that never made sense — 1.7% on Blocks against 70–90% on CitySample. Blocks re-fires contacts densely enough that physics usually catches one between polls; the CitySample surrogate does not.

02 The evidence, in one pair of lines

With the witness running, mid-descent — Unreal reporting contact after contact against an actor resting at 0.759, with a textbook ground normal, while the engine sees nothing:

gate=F-HIT  n=100 object=FastGeoSurrogateActor_0 impact_z=0.959 actor_z=0.759 normal=(0.000,0.000,-1.000)
gate=G-TICK grounded=0 z=2.081 vz=0.695 collided=0

Same fixture, same world, nothing polling the RPC:

gate=C-GROUND-LOCK ENGAGED body=MultiRotorPhysicsBody at z=0.759
gate=G-TICK grounded=1 z=0.759 vz=0.000 collided=1

The flag survives to the physics tick, the lock engages, the landing ends.

03 Why “switch it off” is not the fix

The theft was masking a second defect

With no reader at all, the flag is never cleared, the engine keeps taking the collision path against a contact Unreal re-reports every tick, and the vehicle is pinned to the ground:

gate=G-TICK grounded=0 z=0.803 vz=-2.641 collided=1   ← commanding 2.6 m/s of climb, not moving

Two takeoff timeouts in seven runs, against zero in twelve. Filed as SIM-43. Any fix has to keep someone clearing the flag while stopping the witness being that someone — most likely a non-consuming snapshot for observers, with the engine as sole consumer.

04 Three wrong answers first

Recorded because the pattern is the useful part, and all three were stated with more confidence than they had earned.

ClaimedWhy it was wrong
“Gate B rejects the surface — the normal reads as a wall”True on one seed of six. Generalised from a single measurement, and it produced a C++ patch against a cause that was not the cause. It flew, and changed nothing.
“The ground lock works, the body is static”Compared seed 1’s probe trace against seed 2’s engine log — two different flights, aligned on wall-clock by assumption.
“The engine is never told about the contact”Right that it was not told; wrong about why. The events were arriving and being eaten by our own tool.

The instrumentation was wrong twice as well: one shared throttle timer starved the rare gate so gate=C could never print, and the first release probe named no body, so 171 s of zero-wrench lines could not be attributed to anything.

What actually worked

Refusing to read silence as evidence. Every wrong answer came from treating an absence — no gate C lines, no engine activity, no contact — as if it meant something. The gate=G-TICK probe, which logs unconditionally on every tick whether grounded or not, is what made absence impossible and ended the guessing.
