01 Blocks: a clean pass that proves less than it looks

The gate’s own criterion — SR == 1.0 with zero VOID — met on a 40-sample basis for the first time. Worst-error min 0.778, median 0.786, max 0.829 m, sd 0.013 m.

And the repair fired zero times

Blocks has no World Partition, so the vehicle never falls: ensure_grounded finds it already resting at z ≈ +0.620 and releases. The 40/40 is a real baseline for the flight path and says nothing about SIM-30’s repair, SIM-10’s stale origin, or SIM-27.

03 SIM-27 is not a 1.7% flake

t=136.26  phys_z=28.896  pose_z=0.763  dz=28.13  vz=0.696
t=136.86  phys_z=29.315  pose_z=0.763  dz=28.55  vz=0.693

pose_z frozen — the actor is on the ground, which is what the cameras see and why the video always looked like a normal landing. phys_z still growing at the last sample, at exactly MPC_LAND_SPEED = 0.7.

~28 m is the timeout, not a floor

40 s of descent at 0.7 m/s. The vehicle never touches down, so the split grows until the harness stops asking. The number is the timeout looking back at us. And the ~1.7% figure came from Blocks: the sample-size problem was never the obstacle — the world was.

The surrogate is the same actor that made SIM-32’s streaming gate release 0.4 s early — leading hypothesis, not proven: it carries collision the integrator does not honour as ground. One defect, two faces. Seed 7 is the lever: the only seed with no split was the only one struck by a car (vz = −1.814, ascending). An external impulse appears to break the stuck state.

04 simPause did not fail

Worth stating plainly

Only 1 of 10 seeds needed a ground repair — it caught the vehicle at z = +1159 m and put it back at +0.745 in 125 s. Seeds 4, 5, 6 and 8 split by ~28 m with no repair and no origin restart, so the split happens without simPause ever intervening. That rules out both “the repair causes it” and “the repair failed to prevent it”. SIM-30 fixes the bring-up fall; it never claimed to fix landing, and City Sample is the first place we could see landing was broken at all.

SIM-10 appeared twice — origins 875 m and 975 m out, both repaired by a PX4 restart, both correctly scored VOID, not FAIL. Seed 2 showed on ground at z=+0.000 and still had an origin 875 m out: PX4 had built its EKF during the descent before ensure_grounded ever looked.

05 The correction that qualifies all of it

I flew the wrong scenario in the wrong world, silently

scenarios/square-10m.yaml declares world: …/Blocks.uproject and describes itself as “an empty world. No obstacles by design”. I flew it in City Sample via --world and nothing warned: resolve_world() is cli_world or scenario["world"], with no comparison. Its own docstring says the scenario’s “results mean nothing if it is flown somewhere else” — and the code permits exactly that.

A 10 m square at 20 m altitude, relative to HOME, landing back on a city street. Waypoints being HOME-relative is why it is silent: the square is geometrically valid anywhere, so nothing fails loudly.

What survives

The SIM-27 split and the stale origins. A landing-physics defect and an EKF init defect do not care which square was flown.

What is now suspect

The collision failures, which I first reported as a gate scoring problem. They are at least as much a wrong-pairing artefact I created — filed as SIM-36 alongside SIM-35 (crash vs being walked into) and SIM-34 (the gate cannot record the chase camera).
